Layering Under a Shell

An anorak is only the outside layer. What you wear underneath decides whether you stay warm and dry, or cold and clammy.

02.1

The three-layer idea

Outdoor clothing is usually built in layers: a base layer next to the skin, a mid layer for warmth and an outer shell such as an anorak for wind and rain. Each does one job, and you can add or remove them as conditions change.

02.2

Base layer: move moisture

The base layer’s job is to pull sweat away from your skin. Merino wool and synthetic fabrics do this well. Cotton holds moisture and loses warmth when damp, so it is best avoided on cold or active days.

02.3

Mid layer: trap warmth

The mid layer holds warm air close to your body.

  • Fleece is breathable and warm even when damp.
  • Synthetic insulation packs small and copes with moisture.
  • Down is warmest for its weight in dry cold.

02.4

Shell: block wind and rain

The anorak goes on top. Even a light, windproof shell can make a big difference, because it stops wind from stripping away the warm air your mid layer has trapped. Waterproof shells add protection from steady rain.

02.5

Adjust as you go

The best layering habit is to change before you are uncomfortable. Open the front zip or take off a mid layer before you sweat on a climb, and add a layer as soon as you stop for a break. Our wind chill planner suggests a starting point.
